# Kill Criteria — Peristalsis

**Phase:** 8 — Validation
**Project:** peristalsis
**Date:** 2026-07-23
**Confidence:** High (protects against sunk-cost thinking)

---

Specific, measurable conditions under which the founder should **stop, pivot, or downgrade to a free personal tool.** Each ties to an experiment. "Kill" here means *kill the paid-business ambition* — not necessarily the personal tool (the founder builds it for himself regardless; that's fine and separate).

| # | Kill/Pivot condition | Tied to | Action if hit |
|---|---|---|---|
| K1 | **<3/10** qualified users make a real payment commitment in E1 | A1 WTP | Stop paid build. Keep free/personal or open-source. Do **not** launch paid on verbal-yes. |
| K2 | Launch-window free→paid **<0.5%** across ≥500 signups | A3 | Pivot monetization (patron-only/donation) before assuming subscription works. |
| K3 | 4-week retention **<20%** on the core feed | A9 | The tool isn't sticky even for free users → fix retention before any monetization or sharing build. |
| K4 | Free-tier cost-to-serve **>$0.50/active user/mo** at 10k (E3) with no cheaper path | A6 | Hard-cap free tier or gate sub-count; if that repels the core user, revenue model is broken. |
| K5 | YouTube ships **native subscription grouping + sticky chronological** | A12/R2 | Reassess wedge immediately; pivot to graph/sharing/multi-platform differentiation or wind down paid ambition. |
| K6 | **API C&D or key revocation** AND the API-independent mode isn't ready | A11/R3 | Emergency: flip to OPML/feed-URL-only mode; if unviable, stop commercial operation, seek counsel. |
| K7 | Reliability: sustained **>2% of channels** dropping videos and unfixable within founder bandwidth | A10/R4 | The core promise ("nothing hidden") is broken → the product has no reason to exist; stop until fixed. |
| K8 | By **~M6 post-launch**: <~60 payers / <~$400 MRR **and** flat/declining retention | projections time-box | Downgrade to lifestyle tool; stop investing growth hours (opportunity-cost kill). |

## Guiding Principle
The founder's intrinsic motivation ("I build it for myself") is a strength for *durability* but a **liability for honest evaluation** — it makes it easy to keep pouring hours into a non-business. These criteria exist to force that distinction: **a great personal tool that no one pays for is a success as a tool and a failure as a business.** Decide, per criterion, which one you're running.

---

## Flags

**Red Flags:**
- K1 is the master switch. If it trips, most downstream work (extension, graph, sharing, pricing tiers) should stop — resist the urge to "build a bit more and re-test."

**Yellow Flags:**
- K5/K6 are partly outside the founder's control; pre-building the mitigations (differentiation depth, API-independent mode) is the only hedge.
